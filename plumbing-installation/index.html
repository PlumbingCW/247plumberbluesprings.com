Water service line work

The service line carries water from the meter to your house. When it fails or needs rerouting, the owner is responsible from the meter inward. The City of Blue Springs water line at (816) 622-4444 can confirm where its responsibility ends.

Excavation near a meter should be coordinated with the utility, and locates of buried lines should be requested before digging.

Planning rough-in before walls close

Rough-in is the stage where supply lines, drains and vents are placed inside open walls and floors. It is the cheapest time to make changes. Decide fixture locations, shower valve depth and vanity type first, since each decides where a pipe sits. A last-minute move after drywall costs far more than a conversation before.

Bring your contractor, your plumber and your tile installer into the same discussion. A drain that lands under a floor joist or a valve set too deep for the trim kit are small mistakes that become expensive ones.

Fixtures, shutoffs and the details that prevent callbacks

Every fixture should have its own shutoff valve so a repair does not mean closing the whole house. Use braided stainless supply lines, not thin plastic ones. Add a drain pan under a washing machine on an upper floor and under a water heater in finished space. These steps cost little at installation and prevent leaks later.

Test before you close. We pressure-test supply lines and run water through each drain, then check for leaks at every joint. A short test with the walls open avoids a long one afterward.

Working with an older house

Houses from the 1970s through the 1990s may combine copper, galvanized steel and newer PEX in one system. Connecting new pipe to old needs the right transition fittings, because mixing metals without them speeds corrosion. We look at what is already there, tell you what we find and plan the tie-in.

If the old pipe is in poor shape, a remodel is a sensible time to talk about repiping the affected section instead of connecting new work to failing lines.
