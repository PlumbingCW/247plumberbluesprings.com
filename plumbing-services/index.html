Emergency work and planned work

Emergency work is anything where waiting makes the damage worse, such as a burst pipe, a sewer backup, a failed water heater tank or a sump pump that quit during a storm. For these, call the 24/7 line, follow the steps we give you for the first few minutes and we schedule the earliest visit available.

Planned work, such as a water heater installation, repiping, a water softener or backflow testing, is scheduled at a time that suits you. The call is the same and the price is clear before work starts. We keep emergency and planned pages separate so you can read what applies to you.

A useful rule of thumb is to ask whether water or sewage is moving where it should not. If yes, treat it as an emergency and shut off the main. If a drip, a slow drain or a weak flush has been going on for weeks, it can usually wait for a scheduled visit, though a call still costs nothing.

Some jobs start planned and become urgent. A faucet drip that ignores repairs can turn into a failed supply line. A slow drain can become a backup. If a small problem is getting bigger, call and tell us how it has changed.

Not sure which service you need?

Describe the symptom and we route you. Water on the floor is burst pipe repair. Several drains slow together is sewer line cleaning. A water bill that jumped is leak detection. No hot water is water heater repair.

Our plumbing blog also covers common questions, such as how relief valves work and what a clogged main looks like.

How we help you choose

If you describe the problem on the phone, we tell you which service page fits and what to expect. Many calls start as one problem and turn into another once the plumber looks, such as a slow drain that turns out to be a damaged lateral. We explain the change before any work continues.
