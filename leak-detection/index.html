Emergency Leak Detection in Blue Springs, MO

A hidden leak wastes water every minute and damages what you cannot see. We locate it first and fix only what is leaking.

Call (855) 575-2890

Leak detection in Blue Springs, MO is the process of locating water escaping from a pipe you cannot see, behind a wall, under a floor, in a crawlspace or buried in the yard, without tearing things open to look. If your meter keeps moving with everything off, a ceiling stain is spreading or a floor feels warm, call the 24/7 line.

Shut off the main water valve if water is actively pouring, and turn off power to any wet area you can reach safely. If the leak is small, keep water use to a minimum and call so we can schedule the earliest visit available.

We start with the symptom you notice, because most leak calls begin with a bill, a sound or a stain and not with the words leak detection. Then we narrow it by testing, listening and imaging.

Symptoms that mean you should test for a leak

Many leaks announce themselves long before a pipe fails.

  • The water meter dial keeps turning with every fixture off.
  • Your water bill rose with no change in how you use water.
  • A ceiling or wall stain that grows or smells musty.
  • A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water at night.
  • Low pressure that came on suddenly.
  • A soggy patch in the yard that does not dry.

The meter check is simple. Turn off every fixture, find the small dial or triangle on the meter, and see whether it moves over ten minutes.

Water meter running and high-bill leaks

The City of Blue Springs bills residential water with tiered usage rates set per 100 gallons, so a leak that runs for weeks can show up as a larger jump than you might expect. Call the city water and sewer line at (816) 622-4444 to ask for a meter re-read before assuming the leak is inside your house.

Some southern Blue Springs addresses are served by Jackson County PWSD 13 instead of the city, so check your bill to see which utility reads your meter. If the meter turns while everything is off, the leak is somewhere between the meter and the fixtures, and that is where we work.

Ceiling, wall and crawlspace leaks

A stain on a ceiling is rarely directly under the leak. Water runs along framing before it appears, so we trace the stain back through the plumbing above, test supply lines under pressure and use moisture meters to map the damp area.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks follow the same logic but with a flashlight and a lot of patience. Fixing a small pinhole while the damage is still shallow can cost a fraction of repairing rotted subfloor later.

A leak on the service line between the meter and the house is a water line problem, and that repair is quoted after the location is confirmed.

Buried-line and irrigation leaks

The water service line runs underground from the meter to the house. A break there can waste water for months with no stain anywhere inside. Irrigation systems add more buried pipe, and a stuck zone valve can run all night.

We confirm the line is the problem with a pressure test, then pinpoint it with acoustic equipment so excavation is a small hole and not a trench through the lawn.

Questions we hear from Blue Springs homeowners

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Blue Springs?

Yes. We answer 24/7. If water is actively leaking, shut the main valve and call so we can schedule the earliest visit available.

How do I tell if my meter is showing a leak?

With all water off, watch the meter for ten minutes. If the dial or leak indicator moves, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Will you have to cut into my walls?

Only if testing points to a specific spot. We use tools to narrow the location first, so any opening is as small as we can make it.

Who is responsible for a leak between the meter and my house?

Typically the homeowner. Your utility can confirm where its responsibility ends for your address.

Can a leak raise my water bill without any visible sign?

Yes. A running toilet or a buried line can waste thousands of gallons with no stain or sound.
