Plumber in Historic Downtown Blue Springs, MO

Plumbing help for older homes, small houses and mixed-use blocks around Main Street. Call and describe the problem.

64015

Historic Downtown Blue Springs is the older core of the city, in ZIP code 64015. Blue Springs was founded in 1838 and incorporated in 1880, and its center moved in 1878 to the site of a new railroad station. Real estate listings for the area place houses on streets such as SW Smith Street, SW Walnut Street and SW 18th Street, and one listing describes a home in a historic district.

This page covers the residential side of downtown. It also touches on the Main Street area, where the city has started a reconstruction project. We keep it practical and we do not guess about the pipes under any particular street.

What is documented about the area

A neighborhood data profile for downtown reports that most homes date from 1940 to 1969, with a number built from 1970 to 1999. It describes mostly small to mid-sized single-family houses, some apartment buildings and a mix of owners and renters. Listings we reviewed show two to three bedroom houses of roughly 1,250 to 2,140 square feet.

We do not have a published boundary map. We use the streets above and the Main Street corridor as anchors, and we do not claim an official boundary.

Main Street work and your home

The city started reconstructing Main Street in 2026 and expects work to finish in spring 2027, funded by a 2024 voter-approved bond. Reconstruction also covers 11th Street. A separate mixed-use project with 210 apartments has been reported for a former lumber yard site, though no schedule was given.

Road work does not by itself change what happens inside your house. It is a good time to know where your main water shutoff and sewer cleanout are, and to ask the city who handles a problem if you see water at the curb. The City of Blue Springs water and sewer line is (816) 622-4444.

Older houses: look before you assume

A house from the mid twentieth century may have been updated in pieces over the years, so one bathroom can be modern while another is original. We do not assume what any pipe is made of. The honest way to know is to look, and for a sewer line that means a camera. A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of the line on video and is useful before you buy or after repeated clogs.

If a drain keeps slowing down, start with drain cleaning. If you see a stain or hear water running with nothing on, leak detection is the next step.

Renting or owning near Main Street

Because downtown has both owners and renters, the first call depends on who holds the lease.

  • Renters should report a leak or backup to the landlord or property manager first, in writing.
  • Owners can call us directly and describe what they see.
  • Take a photo of any water damage before you clean up.
  • Know where your main shutoff is before you need it.

If water is spraying, close the main shutoff first and then call.

What is verified and what is not

Verified from public sources: the ZIP code 64015, the street names above, the city founding and incorporation dates, the Main Street reconstruction timing reported in 2026 and the reported 210-unit mixed-use plan. Not verified: exact boundaries, building ages for any one address and the condition of any pipe.

Questions from Historic Downtown Blue Springs homeowners

Is downtown Blue Springs mostly old houses?

Public data suggests many homes date from 1940 to 1969, with others built later. Your own house may differ, so check its age before you assume anything.

Does the Main Street construction affect my plumbing?

We have no information that it does. If you notice water at the curb or low pressure, call the City of Blue Springs water and sewer line at (816) 622-4444.

Should I get a sewer camera inspection before buying a downtown house?

It is a common step for older homes, because it shows the line on video. Ask a plumber what the video shows before you decide.

I rent near Main Street. Who do I call?

Start with your landlord or property manager. You can still call us to ask what a symptom usually means.
