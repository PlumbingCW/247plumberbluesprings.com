Older housing, older pipes

Independence includes neighborhoods that predate the postwar building boom, and their plumbing may be cast iron, clay or galvanized steel. These materials corrode from the inside, crack at joints and admit roots. A cast iron drain that has rusted through can leak sewage into a crawlspace for a long time before you notice.

We use a camera to see the pipe, and we explain whether the answer is a spot repair, a section replacement or trenchless pipe lining.

Water source and hardness

The City of Independence runs the water supply that the City of Blue Springs buys part of. The state-filed 2025 report lists carbonate hardness of 123 mg/L, about 7 grains per gallon by our conversion, which is moderately hard. Scale builds up in heaters and cartridges, so see water heater repair for related symptoms.

Your water bill and meter questions go to the utility that reads your meter, so check your bill first. If the meter spins with everything off, the leak is ours to find.

Older streets, older service lines

In the oldest parts of any city, the water service line from the meter to the house may be older than the house plumbing. Lead, galvanized or old copper service lines can restrict flow and fail without warning. If your pressure has dropped at every tap, the service line may be the cause. We start by checking pressure at an outdoor spigot and then following the line.

A repair or replacement of the owner-side line is quoted once we confirm where it fails.
