How to Install a New Garbage Disposal: What Blue Springs Homes Need to Know

A disposal swap is a manageable project if you know the steps. Here is what the job involves and where it goes wrong.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A garbage disposal installs under the kitchen sink and grinds food waste so it can pass through the drain. A swap of a like-for-like unit is a common repair job. Adding one where none existed takes more planning, because it needs power and a switch.

This guide covers choosing a unit, the steps of installation and the points where a leak or jam is most likely. If you would rather hand it off, our garbage disposal repair page covers what a plumber does on a visit.

The first rule is safety. Turn off the power at the breaker, not just the wall switch, before you touch anything.

Choosing a unit

Disposals are rated by motor horsepower and by feed type. Continuous-feed units run while you add scraps. Batch-feed units run only when a stopper is in place, which some households prefer for safety. A one-third or half horsepower unit suits light use. Three-quarter or one horsepower handles heavier cooking and tougher scraps.

Match the mounting system to your sink. Many manufacturers use a quick-lock mount, and old sinks may need an adapter. Check the flange diameter and whether you have a dishwasher drain connection that needs to be knocked out of the new unit.

Tools and parts to gather

Have these on the counter before you start.

  • Screwdrivers and a hammer.
  • Plumber's putty or the supplied gasket.
  • Channel-lock pliers.
  • A bucket and towels.
  • A power cord kit if the unit does not include one.
  • New drain pipe and a trap if the old one is worn.

Photograph the old connections before you take them apart. It makes reassembly easier.

Installation steps in order

Disconnect power at the breaker. Remove the old unit by loosening the mounting ring and lowering it, disconnecting the drain and the dishwasher hose. Clean the sink flange and reseat it with fresh putty, then fasten the new mounting assembly under the sink. Connect the power cord inside the unit as instructed and lift the new disposal onto the mount.

Rotate the unit until the discharge aligns with the drain, connect the drain and the dishwasher hose and tighten everything. If your dishwasher drains into the disposal, remove the knockout plug inside the unit before use, or the dishwasher will not drain. Run water and check every joint for drips before restoring power.

Where installations go wrong

A forgotten knockout plug blocks the dishwasher. A loose flange leaks onto the cabinet floor. An overtightened ring cracks the housing. A cord not secured under the cover can create an electrical hazard. Take the time to check each step.

If the sink backs up after installation, the issue is probably a partial clog in the line and not the disposal. See drain cleaning.

Keep it running well

Run cold water during and after grinding, avoid grease and fibrous scraps and clean the chamber occasionally with ice and citrus peel or a dedicated cleaner. A unit used properly can last for years.

Septic and municipal sewer

A disposal adds solids to the drain line. On a municipal sewer that is normally fine. On a septic system the extra solids can fill the tank faster and may call for more frequent pumping. Most Blue Springs homes use city sewer, but if your property is on septic, ask the septic service before you install.

Either way, grease and fibrous scraps still belong in the trash.

Noise, vibration and mounting

A disposal that hums loudly or shakes the cabinet is usually mounted loosely or resting on the trap. Check the mounting ring and make sure the drain pipes are supported. Insulation sleeves and a rubber sink baffle reduce noise further.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

Can I install a disposal if I have no outlet under the sink?

Not without wiring. An electrician or plumber with the right license can add a switched outlet.

Do I need a plumber for a disposal swap?

Not always. If you are comfortable with the steps it is manageable. A leak or electrical issue is a reason to call.

Why is my dishwasher not draining after a new install?

The knockout plug inside the disposal inlet was not removed.

How long does a disposal last?

Many last roughly eight to fifteen years depending on use and care.

Is a disposal good for a septic system?

Ask your septic professional. Many Blue Springs homes are on city sewer, so check your service type.
