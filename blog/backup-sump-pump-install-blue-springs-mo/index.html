Installing a Backup Sump Pump: Blue Springs, MO Homeowner Guide

A backup pump is what keeps a basement dry when the power fails during a storm. Here is how the main options work.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A backup sump pump is a second pump that takes over when the primary pump fails or the power goes out. Storms that raise groundwater often interrupt power, so the backup is needed exactly when you cannot rely on the main pump.

The two common types are battery-powered pumps and water-powered pumps. Each has strengths and limits, and the right choice depends on your home, your water supply and how long outages tend to last.

This guide explains how each works, how installation goes and what to maintain. For emergency pump failure, see our sump pump page.

Battery versus water-powered backups

Both work, in different conditions.

TypeHow it worksLimits
Battery backupA deep-cycle battery runs a second pumpBattery capacity limits run time and needs replacement.
Water-powered backupUses city water pressure to pump out the pitNeeds adequate water pressure and uses water.
Combination systemPrimary pump plus battery pump in one pitNeeds a pit large enough for two pumps.

Water-powered units need a public water supply. A home on a private supply may not have enough pressure.

How installation works

The backup pump sits in the same pit as the primary or in its own pit. It has its own float switch set slightly higher than the primary so it only runs if the primary fails or cannot keep up. The discharge usually ties into the existing pipe through a check valve so water does not flow back.

A battery system adds a charger and a battery in a box placed above the floor. We test by lifting each float and watching the discharge. A sound pit with clean floats and a clear discharge pipe is the foundation of both systems.

What to check before you install

These decide the system you need.

  • The pit size and depth.
  • The condition and age of the primary pump.
  • The discharge route and whether it ties into a drain or runs outdoors.
  • Electrical outlet and GFCI protection.
  • How long local outages tend to last.
  • How much groundwater your basement sees.

The discharge should point away from the foundation so the water does not return to the pit.

Maintenance you can do

Test both pumps each spring by pouring water into the pit until the float rises. Clean the pit of silt and debris once a year. Check the battery terminals and replace the battery according to the manufacturer guidance, which is often every few years. Look at the discharge line for blockages at the outlet.

A backup system that has never been tested is a hope, not a plan.

When the pit overflows anyway

In extreme storms the inflow can exceed pump capacity. A larger pump, a second pit or improved exterior drainage may help. If the basement floods even with a working pump, it is worth looking at where the water comes from, such as downspouts, grading and window wells.

Sizing a backup pump

A backup pump does not have to match the primary, but it should handle the inflow your pit sees during a storm. A pump that moves too little water can keep the basement from flooding in a light event and fall behind in a heavy one. Check the pump curve against the height it must lift and the length of the discharge pipe.

A longer or higher discharge reduces flow. We account for it when we size.

Alarms and monitoring

A high-water alarm in the pit gives a warning if both pumps fall behind. Some systems send a text or alert a phone app. If you travel in spring or summer, an alarm is cheap insurance against coming home to a flooded basement.

Test the alarm during your spring check along with the pumps.

Power outages and generators

A battery backup covers hours, not days. If your area sees long outages, a portable generator that can run the primary pump, or a larger battery bank, may be worth considering. Whatever you choose, test it under load before storm season.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

How long will a battery backup pump run?

It depends on the battery, the pump and how often it cycles. Heavy inflow drains a battery faster.

Can I connect my sump discharge to the sewer?

Often not. Ask the City of Blue Springs before connecting.

Is a water-powered backup worth it?

It can be if you have strong water pressure and long outages, but it uses water.

How often should I test my pumps?

At least once each spring, and after any heavy rain event.

Do I need two pits?

Not always. One pit can hold two pumps if it is large enough.
