Hard Water and Tankless Water Heaters in Blue Springs, MO

Scale is the main enemy of a tankless heater. Here is how it forms, how to flush it and how to protect the unit.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A tankless water heater warms water as it flows, passing it through a heat exchanger. Hard water leaves mineral scale on that exchanger, which restricts flow, causes temperature swings and eventually trips error codes.

Because a tankless unit has less water volume and more demanding heat transfer than a tank, scale affects it faster. The good news is that scale is manageable with a yearly flush and, where useful, a softener.

This guide explains what scale does, how to flush the unit and how to decide on a softener.

Signs of scale in a tankless heater

Look for these changes.

  • Water temperature swings or lukewarm output.
  • Reduced flow at the tap.
  • Error codes related to flow or temperature.
  • A rattling or popping sound from the heat exchanger.
  • Longer wait for hot water than before.

These symptoms can also be a gas supply or venting issue. A plumber checks all of them.

How scale forms and why tankless is sensitive

Heating hard water makes dissolved calcium and magnesium come out as scale. In a tank, much of it sinks to the bottom. In a tankless heater, it coats the narrow channels of the heat exchanger and works like insulation, so the unit runs hotter, wastes gas and wears faster.

Most manufacturers recommend periodic descaling. How often depends on the water, the usage and the model. Check your manual and follow its interval.

How a flush works

A flush circulates a descaling solution, often white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved product, through the heat exchanger using a small pump and hoses connected at the service valves. The loop runs for a set time, then the unit is rinsed with clean water.

Many units have isolation valves installed for this reason. If yours do not, a plumber can add them so future flushes are easier. Our tankless water heater page covers service and installation.

Flush only or flush plus softener

The right plan depends on your water and usage.

SituationPlanWhy
Light scale, low useAnnual flushKeeps the exchanger clear.
Moderate hardness, family useFlush plus softener considerationReduces buildup upstream.
Heavy scale historySoftener and regular flushProtects the whole system.

A softener also protects faucets, dishwashers and fixtures. See water softener.

Gas, venting and install checks

Descaling will not fix an undersized gas line or a blocked vent. If your unit short-cycles or throws ignition codes, we check the gas supply and venting too. For a new install, water heater installation covers planning.

Sizing a tankless heater for Missouri winters

Incoming groundwater temperature falls in winter, so a tankless unit has to raise water further to reach the set temperature. That cuts how many gallons per minute it can deliver. A unit that satisfies two showers in July may struggle in January. Ask a plumber to size for winter conditions.

Hard water adds to the problem, since scale reduces efficiency just when the unit is working hardest. A flush before winter is a good habit.

Descaling versus replacing the heat exchanger

A flush clears scale that is soft enough to dissolve. Hard, thick scale that has built up over years may not come off fully, and the heat exchanger may need replacement. Catching it early with a routine flush avoids that, which is why many manufacturers tie warranty terms to maintenance.

Keep a record of each flush with the date and the solution used. If you ever need warranty service, the log helps.

Water treatment options besides a softener

Scale inhibitors and conditioners add a small feed of polyphosphate to reduce scale formation. They do not remove hardness, and their results vary. A salt-based softener removes it. Ask your manufacturer which approach it supports before you buy anything.

A sediment filter ahead of the heater protects the unit from grit that can foul valves and the flow sensor.

Warranty and manufacturer guidance

Many tankless manufacturers specify water quality limits and maintenance schedules in their warranty. Read the paperwork and keep service records. If your hardness is above the stated limit, a softener or conditioner may be required to keep the warranty valid.

What Blue Springs water brings to a tankless unit

The City of Blue Springs buys treated water from Independence, Kansas City and Tri County Water Authority. The state-filed 2025 report lists carbonate hardness of 123 mg/L for Independence water, about 7 grains per gallon by our conversion. That is moderately hard. We do not have verified figures for the other two sources, so test your tap.

Some southern addresses are served by Jackson County PWSD 13. Ask your water provider which supply reaches your meter.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

How often should I flush a tankless heater?

Many manufacturers suggest about once a year. Hard water can justify more often. Follow your manual.

Can I flush it myself?

Yes, with a small pump, hoses and the right solution. A plumber can do it and check the other components.

Will a softener void my warranty?

Usually not, and many manufacturers recommend water treatment in hard areas. Check your warranty.

Is Blue Springs water hard enough to matter?

The Independence component is moderately hard. Test your tap for a real figure.

What is the first sign of scale?

Often temperature swings or reduced flow at the tap.
