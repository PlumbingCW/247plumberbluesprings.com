Water Heater Thermocouple Repair: Blue Springs, MO Homeowner Guide

When a gas water heater pilot will not stay lit, the thermocouple is the usual suspect. Here is how to check it safely.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A thermocouple is a small safety sensor that sits in the pilot flame of a gas water heater. It generates a tiny electrical current when hot, and that current holds the gas valve open. If the pilot goes out, the current stops and the valve closes, so gas cannot flow unburned.

When a thermocouple weakens or the flame no longer reaches it, the pilot drops out as soon as you release the control knob, or it stays lit for a while and then fails. The result is no hot water and a unit that keeps needing to be relit.

This guide explains the symptoms, how to test and replace the part and, most importantly, when to leave it to a plumber. If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.

Signs of a failing thermocouple

Several symptoms point to this part.

  • The pilot lights but goes out when you release the knob.
  • The pilot burns weakly or with a yellow, flickering flame.
  • Hot water works for a day or two, then stops.
  • The burner will not light even with a steady pilot.
  • Soot or discoloration near the pilot assembly.

A yellow, lazy flame may also mean a dirty pilot tube or a venting problem, so the thermocouple is not always the only cause.

How the thermocouple and gas valve work together

The thermocouple is two dissimilar metals joined at a tip. Heat on the tip produces a few millivolts, just enough to hold an electromagnet in the gas control valve. When the pilot is lit and the tip is hot, the valve stays open. When the tip cools, the magnet releases and the valve closes.

Because the part is a safety device, replacing it with the correct length and fitting matters. A part that does not seat properly in the flame will fail again. Many newer water heaters use a flame sensor and an electronic igniter instead, which have their own service steps.

Pilot problems and what to check

The symptom narrows the part.

A new thermocouple on a unit with a venting fault will fail again. We check the flue as part of the diagnosis.

What replacement involves

The tech shuts off the gas, removes the access cover, unscrews the old thermocouple from the gas valve and the pilot bracket and installs a new one in the same position, with the tip in the pilot flame. After relighting, the tech checks that the flame is blue and steady, that the burner lights normally and that there are no leaks at the fittings.

It is a short job when the part is accessible. We pair it with a quick check of the temperature setting, the relief valve and the anode rod, since a unit with a failing thermocouple may be nearing the end of its useful life. If it is, we explain the options and point you to water heater repair or water heater replacement.

Why a DIY attempt is a judgment call

Some homeowners replace a thermocouple themselves. The parts are inexpensive, but the work involves a gas connection, a pilot flame and combustion. If you are not experienced, a gas fitting that is slightly loose can leak. We recommend a plumber for anything that touches the gas line.

If you do try, shut off the gas supply at the unit, wait for it to cool and check every connection with soapy water after relighting. Bubbles mean a leak and the gas must go off again.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

How long does a thermocouple last?

Many last several years, but it varies with use, venting and water conditions.

Can I tell if the thermocouple is bad without tools?

If the pilot will not stay lit after holding the knob for the recommended time, the part is the likeliest cause. A multimeter test confirms it.

Is it dangerous to relight the pilot myself?

Follow the instructions on the unit. If you smell gas, do not try. Call the gas utility.

Do newer heaters have thermocouples?

Many newer models use electronic ignition and flame sensors instead.

Does Blue Springs require a permit to replace the part?

It depends on the work. Ask Community Development about Section 500.130 and show a license if you hire a plumber.
