Water Heater Relief Valve Repair in Blue Springs, MO

The relief valve is a safety device. When it drips or discharges, something in the system needs attention.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A water heater relief valve, often called a temperature and pressure relief valve, opens when pressure or temperature in the tank goes too high. It prevents a tank from rupturing. When it drips or blows off, it is doing its job, but the cause needs to be found.

Common causes are excess thermal expansion in a closed system, a thermostat set too high, a faulty valve or heavy mineral buildup.

This guide explains what each cause looks like and when to call a plumber. If hot water is gushing from the valve pipe, shut off the heater and the cold supply and call.

Why a relief valve drips or discharges

Most causes fall into four groups.

  • Thermal expansion in a closed plumbing system.
  • A thermostat set above about 120 to 130 degrees.
  • A worn or scaled valve seat.
  • Pressure from the city supply higher than the home expects.
  • A failing thermostat or element that overheats the tank.

A valve that drips slowly after the heater runs often points to expansion or a worn seat.

Thermal expansion and the expansion tank

When water heats, it expands. In a home with a check valve or pressure-reducing valve on the incoming line, that extra volume has nowhere to go, so pressure spikes and the relief valve opens. An expansion tank gives the water room.

If your home has a closed system and no expansion tank, the valve will keep dripping and the fix is the tank, not just a new valve. We test the system pressure with a gauge to confirm.

Test the valve safely

Place a bucket under the discharge pipe, lift the test lever for a moment and let it snap back. Water should flow and then stop. If the valve keeps dripping, the seat is dirty or worn. Do not cap or plug the discharge pipe under any circumstance. A blocked relief valve defeats its purpose.

If the valve is very old or the lever is stuck, do not force it. Call a plumber to replace the valve.

When replacement is the answer

A heater that overheats repeatedly, or whose tank is leaking from the body, should be replaced. Water heater replacement covers sizing and what a visit includes.

What the discharge pipe tells you

The pipe that runs from the valve to the floor or a drain carries any discharge away from you. It should end a few inches above the floor or a drain and must not be threaded or capped. A wet floor under the pipe, or a mineral crust on its end, tells you the valve has been releasing water.

Note when the dripping happens, such as only after a shower or only when the heater fires. That timing helps point to expansion or a pressure issue.

Preventing relief valve trouble

Keep the thermostat at a moderate setting, flush the tank each year to clear sediment and have the pressure on your home checked if you see repeated dripping. High supply pressure stresses every fixture, not just the heater. A pressure gauge on an outdoor spigot gives a quick reading.

Replace a relief valve that has been dripping for a long time even if it now seems fine. Mineral deposits often keep it from seating tightly.

When the relief valve is a symptom of an old tank

A tank near the end of its life loses efficiency, and sediment can insulate the burner or element from the water, causing it to overheat. If your tank is more than about ten years old and the relief valve is acting up, the sensible conversation is replacement, not just another valve.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

Is it normal for a relief valve to drip?

A slow drip is a sign that something needs attention. It is not normal for the valve to leak daily.

Can I replace the relief valve myself?

It is a simple part, but it needs the tank drained and the correct rating. If you are unsure, call a plumber.

What temperature should my heater be set to?

Many sources suggest about 120 degrees. Check your manufacturer guidance.

Do I need an expansion tank in Blue Springs?

If your home has a closed system, often yes. We test and advise.

Who do I call if the valve is discharging near a gas heater?

Shut off the gas and call us. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911.
