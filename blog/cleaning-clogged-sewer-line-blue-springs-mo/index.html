Cleaning a Clogged Sewer Line in Blue Springs, MO

When several drains slow together, the clog is in the main. Here is how it is cleared and what to try, and not try, yourself.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A clogged sewer line is a blockage in the main drain that carries all of the house's wastewater out to the city sewer. Because every fixture feeds it, the signs are shared: several drains slow, gurgling at toilets, or sewage at a floor drain.

Cleaning it means getting a tool through the blockage and restoring full flow, then deciding why it formed. A one-time clog from a flushed object is different from a line that clogs every few months because of roots or damage.

This guide explains how to confirm it is the main, what cleaning involves and what to avoid.

Confirm it is the main line

A quick test before you call.

  • Flush a toilet farthest from the main and watch the other fixtures.
  • Run a basement sink and see if the floor drain burbles.
  • Check whether water backs up in the lowest fixture first.
  • Look for a cleanout cap near the house or in the basement.
  • Note whether the problem began suddenly or slowly.

If only one sink is slow, the clog is likely in that branch and not the main.

What cleaning involves

We usually open a cleanout and run a motorized cable with a cutting head through the line. The blade cuts roots and breaks up grease and debris. We follow with water to confirm full flow, and often with a camera to see what is left. The sequence matters because a line that drains is not necessarily a clean line.

For heavy buildup, hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the pipe wall. It is more thorough than cabling, but it needs a pipe in sound condition. A camera inspection first tells us which tool is safe.

What not to do

Chemical drain cleaners are a poor choice for a main line. They sit in a standing pipe, can damage old metal and plastic and are dangerous to the plumber who later opens the line. A plunger helps a toilet but not a main.

Do not run water through a backing-up line to see whether it clears. Every gallon adds to the overflow. If sewage is coming up, stop using water and call.

Keeping it from coming back

If the clog came from roots, expect regrowth unless the pipe is repaired. If it came from grease, change what goes down the kitchen drain. A yearly camera or cable visit can be a good idea for a line with a history. For recurring trouble, sewer line repair may be the real fix.

Cleanouts and why they matter

A cleanout is a capped opening in the drain line that lets a plumber insert a cable or camera directly. A house with a good, accessible cleanout can be cleared quickly. A house with none requires pulling a toilet or going through the roof vent, which takes more time.

If yours is missing or buried, ask about installing one. It is a small investment that pays back the first time you have a main line problem.

After the cleaning: confirming the line is truly clear

A good job ends with proof. We run water through the main for several minutes while watching the lowest fixture and the cleanout, then camera the line when the situation calls for it. A line that drains the first time and clogs again in a week was only partly cleared.

Ask for the camera recording if one was made. It shows the pipe wall, any roots left behind and whether a belly or crack sits farther down the line. That recording is how you decide whether to schedule a repair or simply watch the line.

Costs of waiting

A slow main line rarely fixes itself. Each week of use packs more debris into the narrowed section, and the eventual backup tends to come at the worst time, such as a holiday or a full house. Clearing it early is almost always cheaper than clearing it during an overflow, and it avoids the cleanup a backup causes.

Blue Springs sewer responsibility

The City of Blue Springs water and sewer line is (816) 622-4444. Typically the homeowner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the city connection, and the city for its main. Ask the city if you suspect the main under the street is blocked.

For after-hours public works contact, the 2024 resident guide lists (816) 228-0150. For plumbing permits, Section 500.130 ties them to proof of license.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

Is a clogged main an emergency?

If sewage is backing up, yes. Stop water use and call.

How often should a sewer line be cleaned?

Only as needed. A line with roots or grease issues may need an annual check.

Will a snake damage my pipes?

A skilled operator choosing the right cutter should not. A camera first helps with fragile pipe.

Who is responsible if the city main is blocked?

The city. Call (816) 622-4444 to report it.

Does insurance cover a sewer backup?

Often only with a backup endorsement. Check your policy.
