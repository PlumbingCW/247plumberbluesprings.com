Cast Iron Toilet Flange Repair in Blue Springs, MO

A toilet that rocks or weeps at the base often has a damaged flange. Here is how to tell and what the repair involves.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A toilet flange is the ring that bolts the toilet to the floor and connects it to the drain. On many older homes the flange is cast iron, soldered or caulked to a cast iron drain, and it can corrode, crack or sit too low.

When the flange fails, the wax seal between flange and toilet fails with it. That lets sewer gas and water escape into the floor, often long before a stain shows.

This guide explains the signs, how repairs differ by pipe type and when to call a plumber and not buy a repair kit.

Signs of a bad flange

These point to the flange and not the toilet itself.

  • The toilet rocks or shifts when you sit.
  • Water or moisture around the base after flushing.
  • A sewage odor in the bathroom.
  • A soft or discolored section of floor near the toilet.
  • Closet bolts that will not stay tight.
  • A toilet that flushes weakly because the seal is leaking air.

A rocking toilet should not wait. The wax seal breaks and the leak happens under the floor where you cannot see it.

Why cast iron flanges fail

Cast iron rusts, especially where moisture sits. A flange that has seen years of tiny leaks around the wax ring thins, cracks or breaks at the bolt slots. Rust can also grow inward and narrow the opening.

Flanges also fail because the floor was raised. A new layer of tile can leave the flange below the finished surface, which lets the toilet rock and the seal gap. A spacer or extension ring corrects the height.

A repair ring is a quick fix when the pipe itself is sound. If the pipe below is corroded, a ring will not hold.

Check the subfloor before you reset the toilet

A long-running leak can soften plywood or even rot the joists. We probe the floor around the flange before setting a new bowl. If it is soft, the subfloor needs repair first, or the new flange will loosen again.

Moisture trapped below can also grow mold. Replace damaged sections and let the area dry before closing it up.

Toilet leaking at the base: first steps

Do these if water is on the floor.

Close the supply valve.

Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise.

Do not flush.

Each flush sends water out the leaking seal.

Mop and ventilate.

Dry the floor and open a window.

Call a plumber.

We can reset the toilet and repair the flange.

Related repairs in the same visit

While the toilet is off, replace the supply line and shutoff valve if they are old. These cost little and prevent another leak. Our toilet repair page covers common toilet faults, and drain cleaning covers slow drains that may be related.

Setting the toilet back correctly

Once the flange is sound, the toilet gets a fresh wax ring or a modern waxless seal, new closet bolts and a level seat. The bowl is lowered straight down and not slid sideways, which would smear the ring. The bolts are tightened evenly, a little at a time, to avoid cracking the porcelain.

We test by flushing several times and checking for any leakage at the base before we caulk the edges. A bead of caulk at the front and sides, not the back, lets you see a leak if one starts later.

Preventing flange problems

Use a new wax or waxless seal whenever a toilet is removed. Do not overtighten the closet bolts. If you are changing the floor covering, raise the flange to match the new height. These small steps prevent most flange failures.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

Can I replace a flange myself?

Some owners do. A damaged cast iron flange or soft subfloor is better handled by a plumber, because a mistake leaks under the floor.

Will a repair kit work on cast iron?

Rings are made for this, but they need sound pipe beneath. If the pipe is rusted, the ring will not hold.

How do I know the flange is cast iron?

It is dark, heavy and magnetic. Plastic flanges are white or gray and light.

Is a rocking toilet always a flange problem?

Not always. Loose bolts or an uneven floor can cause it. A plumber checks both.

Is the flange the owner's responsibility in Blue Springs?

Yes, inside the home it is the owner's repair.
