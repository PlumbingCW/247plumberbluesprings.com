Repair a Leaky Bathroom Faucet: Blue Springs, MO Homeowner Guide

Most bathroom faucet leaks come down to a worn cartridge or seal. Here is how to find which and how to fix it.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Blue Springs 24/7 Plumbing team · Published and updated November 14, 2026

A leaky bathroom faucet usually drips from the spout, leaks around the handle or weeps at the base. Each location points to a different part, so the repair starts with finding where the water appears.

Most bathroom faucets are repairable with a cartridge, O-ring or seat. This guide shows how to tell which, what tools you need and when it is smarter to replace the faucet.

If the water is spraying from under the sink and not the faucet itself, shut the angle stops and call. That is a supply leak, not a faucet repair.

Where it leaks and why

The leak location identifies the part.

Leak locationLikely partFix
Drip from spoutCartridge or seatReplace the cartridge.
Leak at handle baseO-ring or packingReplace O-rings.
Leak at faucet baseGasket or mounting sealReseal or replace.
Low flowAerator or depositsClean the aerator.

Fix a dripping spout

Turn off the water at the angle stops under the sink, open the faucet to relieve pressure and plug the drain. Remove the handle, which usually has a small cap hiding a set screw. Pull the cartridge out, noting its orientation, and take it to a hardware store to match the replacement.

Install the new cartridge in the same orientation, reassemble and turn the water on slowly. If the drip continues, the valve seat may be worn and needs resurfacing or the faucet replaced.

Tools and parts to gather

Having them ready saves a trip.

  • Small screwdrivers and an allen key set.
  • Adjustable wrench.
  • Replacement cartridge or O-ring kit.
  • Plumber's grease.
  • A towel to protect the sink.
  • White vinegar for cleaning scale.

Take photos at each step so you can reassemble in order.

When to call a plumber

If the handle will not come off, the shutoff valves will not turn or the leak continues after a new cartridge, call a plumber. A corroded angle stop can break when forced and turn a drip into a flood. Faucet repair covers what we do and how a visit works.

If the faucet is more than 15 years old, replacement is often the better value than hunting for discontinued parts.

Hard water and bathroom faucets

Mineral scale builds up inside cartridges and aerators and wears seals faster. If scale is visible on the spout, soak the aerator in vinegar and clean it. A water softener reduces scale throughout the house.

Tub and shower faucets are a separate job

A dripping tub spout or shower head usually points to the valve behind the wall, not the visible trim. Many single-handle shower valves use a cartridge that pulls out from the front once the handle and trim plate are off. Two-handle valves use stems that can be replaced the same way.

If you cannot reach the valve without opening the wall, or the shutoff for the bathroom does not exist, call a plumber. Opening a wall is simple when there is an access panel and a large job when there is not.

How to avoid a flood while you work

Close the angle stops under the sink and open the faucet to confirm the water is off before you disassemble anything. Place a towel in the sink so a dropped screw does not fall down the drain. Keep small parts in a cup in the order you removed them.

If a stop valve will not close or drips after you shut it, stop. A seized valve is a repair of its own, and forcing it can break the stem. Close the main and call for help.

Parts that stay the same and parts that do not

Faucet brands use different cartridge shapes, and a part that looks right may not seat properly. Take the old cartridge with you or note the brand and model stamped on the body. Many faucets also show a code on the underside of the handle.

Avoid generic parts for a faucet you want to keep a long time. The right cartridge costs a little more and lasts longer.

Blue Springs water and your faucet

The City of Blue Springs buys treated water from Independence, Kansas City and Tri County Water Authority. The state-filed 2025 report lists carbonate hardness of 123 mg/L for the Independence supply, about 7 grains per gallon by our conversion, which is moderately hard. Your tap can differ, so test it.

The city bills water by usage tiers per 100 gallons, so a steady drip adds to the bill over time. The city water and sewer line at (816) 622-4444 can answer billing questions.

Questions about this topic in Blue Springs

How much water does a dripping faucet waste?

A faucet that drips steadily can waste thousands of gallons a year, depending on the rate.

Do I need to shut off the main water?

Usually the angle stops under the sink are enough. If they will not turn, shut the main.

Why does my faucet drip only when hot?

The hot side cartridge or seat is likely the worn one.

Does Blue Springs water shorten faucet life?

Moderately hard water leaves scale that wears seals. Regular aerator cleaning helps.

Should I replace the whole faucet?

If it is old, corroded or parts are unavailable, yes. Otherwise a cartridge is cheaper.
