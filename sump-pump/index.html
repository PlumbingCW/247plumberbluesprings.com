A pump that runs nonstop may be stuck on a failed float or overwhelmed by inflow. One that does not run at all may have lost power or burned out.

Pump symptoms and what they mean

The behavior of the pump narrows the cause.

SymptomLikely causeAction
Does not startPower loss, stuck float or dead motorTest power and float, replace if burned out.
Runs nonstopStuck float, failed check valve or heavy inflowFree the float, check valve, inspect inflow.
Short cyclesCheck valve backflow or float settingReplace valve, adjust float.
Loud rattlingDebris in the impeller or worn bearingsClean the pit, replace pump if worn.
Runs but moves little waterClogged discharge or frozen lineClear the line.

A pump should discharge away from the foundation. A discharge pipe that dumps near the wall can send the water straight back.

Why Blue Springs basements take on water

Blue Springs sits in the Jackson County, and heavy spring rain can raise groundwater around foundations. Older basements built in the 1970s to 1990s growth decades may lack interior drainage or a pit.

A sump pit collects water from beneath the slab and the pump sends it outdoors. Without it, the water finds the lowest point in the house.

Battery backup and maintenance

Storms that fill the pit often knock out power too. A battery backup pump keeps running when the main pump cannot. We test the system by lifting the float and watching the discharge.

Test the pump at the start of spring by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Clean the pit of silt and debris annually.

Pump maintenance checklist

A few minutes a year prevents most failures.

  • Pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and drains it.
  • Confirm the discharge line is clear and routes away from the house.
  • Check that the check valve is installed and working.
  • Clean silt from the pit.
  • Replace a pump that is more than about seven to ten years old.

These intervals are common guidance and not a guarantee. A pump under heavy use wears sooner.
