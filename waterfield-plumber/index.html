Plumbing in established detached homes

A house from the 1980s or 1990s is old enough that original parts are worth a look. Many tank water heaters wear out somewhere around eight to twelve years, so a heater that came with the house or has been replaced once may be near the end of its life. This is general guidance and not a statement about Waterfield. Faucet cartridges, toilet fill valves and supply shutoff valves also wear with use.

If your water heater is leaking, see water heater repair or water heater replacement. If a faucet drips or a fixture is slow to drain, faucet repair and drain cleaning cover the usual fixes.

A quick look around a basement home

Many homes in this part of Blue Springs have basements, so a few minutes of checking can catch trouble early.

  • Look at the base of the water heater for rust streaks or a damp floor.
  • Find the main water shutoff and turn it once a year so it does not seize.
  • Check any floor drain for slow draining or odors.
  • Look at the ceiling and walls near bathrooms for new stains.
  • If you have a sump pump, pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts.

A stain that grows or a smell that returns is a reason to call instead of waiting.

Finishing a basement bathroom

If your basement already has a capped bathroom rough-in, finishing it is a plumbing project with real steps. The drain slope, the vent, the supply lines and a pressure test all matter, and a finished wall hides mistakes. Ask the city's Community Development office whether the work needs a permit and confirm that your plumber can show proof of license. Our plumbing installation page explains how an installation call works.

If the basement has ever taken on water, read about sump pump service before you finish any walls.
